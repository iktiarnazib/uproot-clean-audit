Ranked by how many ads and variants you're running on each. An angle that keeps getting new variants is one the media buyer is confident in.

Redirects the blame to a hidden cause (biofilm), explains how it works in plain language, offers one simple fix, and backs it with the 60-day guarantee. 62+ ads.

"The smell isn't on your clothes. It's inside your washing machine."

② The repair-tech expert Scaling

A technician with "19 years, 6,000 machines" takes a washer apart on camera. These are long-form (3–6 min) videos built on authority and reveal. Several versions are running (Danny in Chicago, the Jacksonville van tech).

③ Side-by-side test Strong proof

Vinegar vs. Affresh vs. Uproot in the same machine on the same cycle. The dark drain water is the moment that sells it. 9 ads, 47 placements, launched Sep 28, so it's still being scaled.

"…the water that drained out was seriously dark."

Calls out Affresh/Whirlpool by name: "Whirlpool owns Affresh. Whirlpool also sold you the front-loader." It creates a clear enemy and a reason to switch.

Rashes on a child, breakouts on the jawline, "protecting your lungs," and "NASA-inspired enzymes." For the hair remover, it's the musty smell that comes from the carpet, not the dog.

05Where I see room to grow

These are gaps I noticed from the outside. I'd validate them against your ROAS data before acting on any of them.

  1. The hero product gets older creative. The washer has expert, test and teardown formats, while the Cleaner Pro mostly runs the 2025 "MIRACLE tool" demo and one "musty carpet" ad. Bringing the washer's mechanism-plus-proof structure to the Pro (for example, "Why your vacuum can't get this hair," with a macro shot of hair stuck sideways in carpet fibers, then the scrape) looks like the biggest available win.
  2. Long-form winners need more short versions. The 3–6 minute teardowns are great for authority, but each one has 5–10 strong moments (panel comes off, gunk revealed, dark water). Each moment can become a hook for its own 15–30 second ad.
  3. Turn the statics into video. The "Apology" and "Official Statement" statics have a strong voice. Acted as a short skit with a deadpan spokesperson reading the "complaints," they'd make cheap, scroll-stopping video.
  4. Q4 gifting. Last December you ran "The perfect gift for any pet owner." With Black Friday and the holidays close, gift-framed edits of the Starter Kit and Complete Package (unboxing, a "for the dog mom who has everything" hook) could raise order value.
  5. More hooks per winner. Swapping just the first 3 seconds of a proven body is the cheapest test in paid social. Here are five I'd test on the top washer ad:
    • "Your washer is lying to you." (dark-water reveal in the first frame)
    • "I made my husband smell the washer door. Big mistake."
    • "POV: you rewash the dog towels for the third time this week"
    • "Repair techs hate when I show you this." (panel off, cold open)
    • "Vinegar did nothing. This did." (split-screen)
